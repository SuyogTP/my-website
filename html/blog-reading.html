Books I Love — by Suyog Basnet

Reading is a cornerstone of my daily routine. For me, books aren't just entertainment—they are frameworks for thinking, tools for building consistency, and windows into fascinating worlds. I find that the ideas I absorb through reading directly feed into how I approach coding and problem-solving.

Here are some of the primary genres, books, and articles that continuously spark my curiosity:

1. Personal Development & Philosophy

I gravitate toward books that offer actionable insights for building better systems in life.

  • Atomic Habits by James Clear: Easily one of the most impactful books I have read. It taught me how small, incremental shifts in behavior lead to compound success. You can read my full breakdown of it on my Atomic Habits Lessons page.
  • Deep Work by Cal Newport: A guide on how to cultivate intense focus in a world full of digital distractions—incredibly useful for long programming sessions.

2. Fiction & Short Stories

When I am not reading to learn, I read to explore. Short stories are a favorite medium because they deliver powerful, creative ideas efficiently.

  • Sci-Fi Anthologies: I enjoy thought-provoking stories that explore speculative future tech, artificial intelligence, and existential concepts.
  • Classic Short Fiction: Pieces that offer tight storytelling, deep character studies, and impactful narrative twists in just a few pages.

3. Science Articles & Tech Essays

To keep my mind sharp and stay up-to-date with emerging ideas, I dedicate time each week to online long-form articles.

  • Scientific Discovery: Articles covering astrophysics, neuroscience, or complex systems that challenge how I view the natural world.
  • Software Engineering Blogs: Deep dives into how engineering teams solve high-scale technical infrastructure challenges.

Conclusion

My reading list is constantly evolving. I believe the best way to maintain a steady reading habit is to remain ruthlessly curious and always keep a book within arm's reach.
