Arduino Uno & Electronics Guide

A simple overview of microcontrollers, components, and concepts.

Arduino Uno

The Arduino Uno is a series of open-source single-board microcontrollers based on a diverse range of microcontrollers (MCU).

Resistors & Ohm's Law

Resistors: They are electronic components that limit the flow of electrical current in a circuit.

Ohm's Law: V = I × R
  • V = Voltage
  • I = Current
  • R = Resistance

Arduino Pin Types

Digital Pins

Send only two states: 0 volts (LOW) or 5 volts (HIGH). They act as output-only on standard pins and cannot read variable incoming data.

Hybrid Pins (PWM / Pulse Width Modulation)

Marked with a tilde symbol (~) on the board. They act as a hybrid by outputting simulated varying voltages through rapid pulsing. They can send varied signals, but are not true analog outputs.

Analog Pins

Read incoming continuous voltage levels (not just 0 or 5V). They accept whole numbers and decimal values from sensors and convert variable input voltages into digital numbers using an internal analog-to-digital converter.

Potentiometer

Acts as a manual variable resistor to change voltage levels in a circuit. It turns physical rotation into a changing voltage signal and is used to control output devices like dimming an LED or changing motor speed.

Buzzers

A buzzer is an audio signaling device that converts electrical energy into a buzzing or beeping sound. There are two types:

  • Active Buzzer: Uses a DC circuit. It cannot be pulsed or turned off internally via simple frequency modulation (produces a continuous tone when powered).
  • Passive Buzzer: Uses an AC circuit. It can be turned on and off to play different frequencies/tones.
